# Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS) — DRAFT, clinician review pending

POTS is a form of dysautonomia where standing up causes an abnormally large increase in heart rate, often with lightheadedness, fatigue, and other symptoms. It disproportionately affects young women.

## Typical presentation

- Heart rate increases by ≥30 bpm (≥40 in adolescents) within 10 minutes of standing
- Lightheadedness, dizziness, or feeling about to faint when upright
- Brain fog, difficulty concentrating
- Fatigue, often profound
- Exercise intolerance
- Headaches
- Nausea, GI symptoms
- Often follows a viral illness, surgery, pregnancy, or major stressor

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms dismissed as "anxiety" — the symptoms can be similar but POTS is a measurable physiological condition
- Diagnosis can take years; many patients see multiple providers first
- POTS often co-occurs with hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (hEDS) and Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS) — clusters worth screening together
- "Normal" resting heart rate doesn't rule out POTS — the change with standing is what matters
- Symptoms often worsen with heat, large meals, dehydration, menstruation

## Tests to ask for

- Active stand test (10 minutes) with serial blood pressure and heart rate measurements
- Tilt table test if available
- Bloodwork: CBC, comprehensive metabolic panel, thyroid panel, ferritin, vitamin B12, vitamin D, morning cortisol
- Referral to a cardiologist or autonomic specialist familiar with POTS
- Echocardiogram if structural heart disease needs to be ruled out

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "I'd like to do an active stand test — can we measure my heart rate and blood pressure standing vs. lying down?"
- "Could my symptoms be dysautonomia rather than anxiety? How would we tell the difference?"
- "Is there a POTS or autonomic specialist you can refer me to?"
- "Are there non-medication strategies (salt/fluid intake, compression, exercise programs) I should try alongside any medications?"
