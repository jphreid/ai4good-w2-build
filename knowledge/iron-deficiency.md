# Iron Deficiency (with or without anemia) — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Iron deficiency can cause significant symptoms *before* it shows up as anemia on a standard blood count. Because the basic CBC can look normal, low iron is frequently missed — especially in menstruating people.

## Typical presentation

- Fatigue and low stamina
- Shortness of breath or a racing heart on exertion
- Pale skin, brittle nails, hair shedding
- Headaches, dizziness, difficulty concentrating
- Restless legs, especially at night
- Unusual cravings for ice or non-food items (pica)
- Heavy periods as a common underlying cause

## Often-missed signs

- A "normal" hemoglobin/CBC while **ferritin** (iron stores) is low — symptoms can occur before anemia
- Fatigue attributed to stress, low mood, or "being busy" without checking iron
- Heavy menstrual bleeding not connected to the low iron it causes
- Ferritin in the low-normal range dismissed even when symptomatic

## Tests to ask for

- **Ferritin** (the key test for iron stores) — not just hemoglobin
- Full iron studies: serum iron, transferrin/TIBC, transferrin saturation
- CBC (to see if anemia is present)
- If heavy periods or GI symptoms: workup for the source of blood loss
- CRP alongside ferritin (inflammation can falsely raise ferritin)

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Can we check my ferritin, not just my hemoglobin?"
- "Could my fatigue be low iron even if I'm not anemic?"
- "If my periods are heavy, should we address that as the cause?"
- "What's the plan — diet, oral iron, or infusion — and how will we recheck?"
