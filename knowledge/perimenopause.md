# Perimenopause / Early Menopause — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Perimenopause is the transition leading up to menopause, when hormone levels fluctuate. It can start years earlier than people expect (often in the 40s, sometimes 30s), and its wide-ranging symptoms are frequently misattributed to stress, depression, or "getting older."

## Typical presentation

- Changes in periods — closer together, farther apart, heavier, or lighter
- Hot flashes and night sweats
- Sleep disruption
- Mood changes, irritability, anxiety, low mood
- Brain fog, trouble concentrating
- Vaginal dryness, lower libido
- New or worsening migraines; joint aches

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms in the late 30s/early 40s dismissed as "too young" for perimenopause
- Mood and cognitive symptoms treated as depression/anxiety alone without considering hormones
- Hormone blood tests can be normal or fluctuate wildly — diagnosis is largely clinical, based on symptoms and cycle changes, not a single lab
- Early/premature menopause (before 40) missed entirely

## Tests to ask for

- A symptom and menstrual-cycle history (the primary basis for diagnosis)
- FSH and estradiol may be checked, but understand they fluctuate — a normal result doesn't rule perimenopause out
- Thyroid panel (TSH) and ferritin to rule out overlapping causes of fatigue/mood/cycle changes
- If under 40 with menopausal symptoms: workup for premature ovarian insufficiency

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Could this be perimenopause, even in my late 30s/early 40s?"
- "Since hormone levels fluctuate, can we go by my symptoms and cycle pattern rather than one blood test?"
- "Can we rule out thyroid and iron issues that look similar?"
- "What are the options — including non-hormonal — and the evidence and risks of each?"
