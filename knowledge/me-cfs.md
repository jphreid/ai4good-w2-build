# ME/CFS (Myalgic Encephalomyelitis / Chronic Fatigue Syndrome) — DRAFT, clinician review pending

ME/CFS is a serious, long-term illness marked by profound fatigue that isn't relieved by rest, and a hallmark crash after exertion. It is frequently dismissed as deconditioning, depression, or "just being tired."

## Typical presentation

- Disabling fatigue lasting months, a clear drop from previous functioning
- Post-exertional malaise (PEM) — symptoms worsen 12–48h after even minor physical or mental effort, and recovery is slow
- Unrefreshing sleep
- Cognitive problems ("brain fog") — memory, processing speed, word-finding
- Orthostatic intolerance — feeling worse on standing (overlaps with POTS)

## Often-missed signs

- PEM is the distinguishing feature and is often not asked about — "exercise more" advice can make ME/CFS worse
- Bloodwork and imaging usually look normal, which is wrongly taken to mean "nothing is wrong"
- Dismissed as depression, though the pattern (PEM, normal mood between crashes) differs
- Frequently co-occurs with POTS, hypermobility, and mast cell issues

## Tests to ask for

- A clinical assessment against established criteria (IOM 2015 / Canadian Consensus) — there is no single confirmatory blood test
- Workup to rule out mimics: thyroid (TSH), ferritin, B12, celiac, sleep study, basic autoimmune screen
- Orthostatic vitals or tilt-table if standing makes things worse

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Can we assess this against the IOM/CCC criteria, including post-exertional malaise?"
- "What can we rule out first — thyroid, anemia, sleep, autoimmune?"
- "What's the evidence on pacing vs. graded exercise for this condition?"
- "Could orthostatic intolerance (POTS) be part of this?"
