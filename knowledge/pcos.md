# Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) — DRAFT, clinician review pending

PCOS is a hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. It is one of the most common causes of irregular periods and infertility, yet diagnosis is often delayed by years.

## Typical presentation

- Irregular or absent menstrual periods (cycles longer than 35 days, or fewer than 8 periods/year)
- Signs of higher androgen levels: adult acne, hair growth on face/chest/back, scalp hair thinning
- Weight gain, especially around the abdomen, that is hard to lose
- Difficulty getting pregnant
- Sometimes: dark, velvety patches of skin (acanthosis nigricans) on the neck or armpits

## Often-missed signs

- "Normal-weight" PCOS — many patients don't gain weight and are told they "don't fit the profile"
- Insulin resistance without diabetes — fasting glucose can be normal while insulin is high
- Mood symptoms (depression, anxiety) that get treated alone without checking the underlying hormones
- Sleep apnea — more common in PCOS than people expect

## Tests to ask for

- Blood work: total testosterone, free testosterone, DHEA-S, SHBG
- Fasting insulin + fasting glucose (HOMA-IR if your provider calculates it)
- Lipid panel
- Thyroid panel (TSH, free T4) — to rule out thyroid causes of irregular periods
- Pelvic ultrasound (transvaginal if appropriate) to look at the ovaries
- Vitamin D, ferritin — commonly low

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Could this be PCOS even though my periods are sometimes regular?"
- "Can we check insulin, not just glucose?"
- "What lifestyle and medication options do you recommend, and what's the evidence for each?"
- "Should I see an endocrinologist or stay with my primary care provider?"
