# Lupus (Systemic Lupus Erythematosus, SLE) — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Lupus is a chronic autoimmune disease where the immune system attacks the body's own tissues. Symptoms come and go ("flares") and affect many organs, which makes it hard to pin down and easy to misattribute.

## Typical presentation

- Persistent fatigue
- Joint pain and swelling
- A butterfly-shaped rash across the cheeks and nose; rashes worsened by sun
- Fevers without an obvious infection
- Hair loss, mouth ulcers
- Sensitivity to sunlight
- Sometimes: chest pain, kidney problems, Raynaud's (fingers changing color in cold)

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms wax and wane, so they're dismissed between flares
- Fatigue and joint pain attributed to stress or "it's all in your head"
- A positive ANA is common but not specific — needs the full clinical picture, not a single test
- Frequently affects women of reproductive age and is under-recognized, especially early

## Tests to ask for

- ANA (antinuclear antibody) as a starting screen
- If ANA positive: anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith, complement levels (C3/C4), ESR/CRP
- CBC (lupus can lower blood counts), urinalysis (to check kidney involvement)
- Referral to rheumatology for diagnosis against the ACR/EULAR criteria

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Given these symptoms, should we screen for autoimmune disease including lupus?"
- "If my ANA is positive, what follow-up antibodies and organ checks do we do?"
- "Can we check my kidneys (urinalysis) and blood counts?"
- "Is a rheumatology referral warranted?"
