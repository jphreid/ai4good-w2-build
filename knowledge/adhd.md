# ADHD — Often Missed in Adults — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder is frequently missed in adults of any gender when it shows up as inattentiveness and internal restlessness rather than the visible hyperactivity people expect. Many are diagnosed only in adulthood, often after a child is diagnosed. (The inattentive presentation is especially under-recognized in girls and women, but it is missed across all groups.)

## Typical presentation

- Trouble starting, organizing, or finishing tasks; chronic procrastination
- Losing track of time, missing deadlines, frequently late
- Forgetfulness, losing things, mental "clutter"
- Difficulty focusing on tasks that aren't immediately interesting
- Emotional intensity, rejection sensitivity, overwhelm
- Coping by overworking or masking — exhausting to maintain

## Often-missed signs

- Labeled "anxious," "scattered," or "not living up to potential" rather than assessed for ADHD
- Symptoms attributed to stress, personality, or life circumstances
- Masking that hides struggle until a life change (new job, parenthood, major transition) removes the scaffolding
- Co-occurring anxiety or depression treated alone while ADHD is missed underneath

## Tests to ask for

- A structured adult ADHD assessment (clinical interview + validated rating scales, e.g. ASRS)
- A developmental history (symptoms present, even if subtle, in childhood)
- Screening for co-occurring anxiety, depression, sleep issues
- Thyroid and iron labs to rule out other causes of fatigue/inattention

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Could this be ADHD presenting as inattentiveness rather than hyperactivity?"
- "Can we use a structured assessment rather than a quick impression?"
- "How do we tell ADHD apart from (or alongside) anxiety and depression?"
- "What are the medication and non-medication options, and the evidence for each?"
