# Adenomyosis — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Adenomyosis is a condition where tissue similar to the uterine lining grows into the muscular wall of the uterus. It causes heavy, painful periods and is frequently confused with — or missed alongside — endometriosis and fibroids.

## Typical presentation

- Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- Severe menstrual cramps that may worsen over time
- Chronic pelvic pain, pressure, or a feeling of fullness
- An enlarged or tender uterus
- Pain during intercourse
- Often co-occurs with endometriosis and/or fibroids

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms dismissed as "normal bad periods" for years
- Confused with fibroids on a quick scan; the two can coexist
- Often only considered after childbearing years, though it occurs earlier too
- Heavy bleeding leading to iron deficiency that gets treated alone without finding the cause

## Tests to ask for

- Pelvic exam (uterus may feel enlarged/tender)
- Transvaginal ultrasound — first-line imaging
- Pelvic MRI — more sensitive for adenomyosis, useful when the picture is unclear
- Ferritin / CBC if bleeding is heavy (to check for iron deficiency)
- Referral to gynecology

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Could this be adenomyosis, not just heavy periods or fibroids?"
- "Would an MRI add clarity if the ultrasound is inconclusive?"
- "Could I have endometriosis or fibroids at the same time?"
- "What are the management options, and which preserve fertility if I want that?"
