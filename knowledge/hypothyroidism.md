# Hypothyroidism / Hashimoto's — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Hypothyroidism is an underactive thyroid — the gland doesn't make enough thyroid hormone. The most common cause is Hashimoto's thyroiditis, an autoimmune condition. Symptoms come on slowly and are easy to attribute to stress, aging, or low mood.

## Typical presentation

- Fatigue that doesn't improve with rest
- Weight gain or trouble losing weight
- Feeling cold when others are comfortable
- Dry skin, brittle nails, thinning or shedding hair
- Constipation
- Heavy, irregular, or prolonged periods
- Low mood, "brain fog," difficulty concentrating

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms dismissed as depression or "just stress" without checking thyroid labs
- A "normal" TSH that sits at the high end while symptoms persist — some people feel unwell before TSH crosses the lab cutoff
- Antibodies (Hashimoto's) present for years before TSH changes
- Co-occurs with other autoimmune conditions (celiac, type 1 diabetes, B12 deficiency)

## Tests to ask for

- TSH and free T4
- Free T3 (in some cases)
- Thyroid antibodies: TPO antibodies, thyroglobulin antibodies (for Hashimoto's)
- Ferritin, vitamin B12, vitamin D — commonly low alongside
- Celiac screen if there are gut symptoms

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Can we check thyroid antibodies, not just TSH?"
- "My TSH is in range but I still have symptoms — what are the options?"
- "Could a deficiency (iron, B12, D) be adding to how I feel?"
- "Should this be rechecked in a few weeks, since thyroid levels shift?"
