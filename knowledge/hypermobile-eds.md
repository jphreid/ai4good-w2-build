# Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (hEDS) / Hypermobility Spectrum — DRAFT, clinician review pending

hEDS is a connective-tissue condition where joints are unusually flexible and tissues are fragile. It is often labeled "just being double-jointed" for years before the wider pattern is recognized.

## Typical presentation

- Joints that bend beyond the normal range; frequent sprains, subluxations, or dislocations
- Chronic joint and muscle pain
- Soft, stretchy, or easily bruised skin; slow wound healing
- Fatigue and poor sleep
- Frequently co-occurring: POTS (dizziness on standing), GI problems, anxiety
- Family history of similar flexibility or pain

## Often-missed signs

- Hypermobility dismissed as a harmless party trick rather than connected to pain, fatigue, and autonomic symptoms
- The cluster (hEDS + POTS + GI/mast cell issues) treated as separate, unrelated complaints by different specialists
- Pain taken less seriously because joints "look fine" on X-ray
- Years of "growing pains" or "clumsiness" in the history

## Tests to ask for

- Beighton score (a quick joint-flexibility assessment) plus the 2017 hEDS diagnostic criteria
- A careful personal and family history
- Referral to genetics or rheumatology if criteria or red flags suggest a specific EDS subtype
- Screen for co-occurring POTS (orthostatic vitals) and GI issues if present

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Can we check my Beighton score and the hEDS criteria?"
- "Could my dizziness/GI issues be connected to hypermobility (POTS, dysautonomia)?"
- "What's the plan for pain and joint stability — physio, bracing, pacing?"
- "Is a genetics or rheumatology referral appropriate?"
