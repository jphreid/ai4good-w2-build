# Endometriosis — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus. It can cause significant pain and infertility. Average time from first symptoms to diagnosis is often reported as 7–10 years.

## Typical presentation

- Painful periods (dysmenorrhea) that interfere with daily life — not just "bad cramps"
- Pelvic pain that occurs outside the menstrual period
- Pain with sex (dyspareunia), often deep
- Pain with bowel movements or urination, especially during periods
- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Infertility or difficulty getting pregnant
- Fatigue, bloating ("endo belly"), nausea around periods

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms dismissed as "normal period pain" — pain that disrupts school, work, or sleep is not normal
- Bowel and bladder symptoms cyclical with periods (often misdiagnosed as IBS or interstitial cystitis)
- Pain that started in adolescence and got progressively worse
- Endometriosis can exist without any visible findings on ultrasound — normal imaging does not rule it out
- Adenomyosis (a related condition affecting the uterine wall) often co-occurs

## Tests to ask for

- Detailed pelvic exam, including assessment for tender nodules
- Transvaginal ultrasound (operator skill matters — ask for a center with expertise in deep endometriosis)
- MRI of the pelvis if deep endometriosis is suspected
- Referral to a gynecologist with endometriosis expertise (not all gynecologists specialize in this)
- Laparoscopy remains the only way to definitively diagnose, but it is not always required to begin treatment

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "My periods disrupt my life. Can we look into endometriosis even though basic imaging was normal?"
- "What treatments are available, and what are the risks and benefits of each?"
- "Is there an endometriosis specialty center you can refer me to?"
- "How will we measure whether treatment is working?"
