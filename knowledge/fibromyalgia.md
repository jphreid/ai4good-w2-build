# Fibromyalgia — DRAFT, clinician review pending

Fibromyalgia is a chronic condition characterized by widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, sleep, and cognitive symptoms. It is more common in women and is frequently dismissed or misattributed.

## Typical presentation

- Widespread pain — typically on both sides of the body, above and below the waist, present for at least 3 months
- Profound fatigue — unrefreshing sleep, waking exhausted
- Cognitive symptoms ("fibro fog") — difficulty with focus, memory, word-finding
- Headaches, including migraines
- Stiffness, especially in the morning
- Sensitivity to touch, temperature, sound, light
- Often co-occurring: IBS, anxiety, depression, restless legs

## Often-missed signs

- Symptoms attributed to "stress" or "depression" alone, without exploring physical components
- Pain that is real and measurable on validated instruments, even when imaging and bloodwork look normal
- Many patients have been told "your tests are normal, so nothing is wrong" — this is not a fair conclusion
- Fibromyalgia often co-occurs with other conditions (hypothyroidism, autoimmune disease, sleep apnea) — these should be ruled out, not assumed to be the same thing

## Tests to ask for

- The 2016 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) diagnostic criteria — based on widespread pain index and symptom severity score
- Bloodwork to rule out other causes: CBC, ESR/CRP, TSH, vitamin D, vitamin B12, ferritin, ANA (if autoimmune disease is suspected)
- Sleep study if sleep is unrefreshing — to rule out sleep apnea or other disorders
- Referral to a rheumatologist if diagnostic clarity is needed

## Questions to bring to the doctor

- "Can we use the ACR criteria to evaluate whether this is fibromyalgia?"
- "What can we rule out first — thyroid, autoimmune, vitamin deficiencies, sleep disorders?"
- "What treatments have the best evidence — including non-medication approaches like graded exercise, CBT, sleep hygiene?"
- "If medications are part of the plan, what should I expect and how will we know if they're working?"
